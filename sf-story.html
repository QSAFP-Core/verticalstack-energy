Why Boundless San Francisco

A funder's mission that already matches how we built this.

Boundless San Francisco, presented in partnership with Crankstart, offers grant funding to organizations with credible ideas for making San Francisco a better place to live. Crankstart is a San Francisco-based family philanthropy devoted to the Bay Area, working toward what it describes as "a vibrant, thriving region whose prosperity is shared by all its residents."

That framing isn't a stretch for VTCNZE — it's close to a literal description of the WIMBY Factor, the profit-sharing structure already built into every Stack SPV. Host communities take the first 40% of distributable profits, ahead of federal, state, city, and investor tranches, administered through Consumer Earned Tokenized Equities (CETEs). Shared prosperity, in this model, isn't a mission statement — it's a cap table.

Where We're Looking

Real grid infrastructure. Real conversations to be had.

San Francisco's brownfield inventory is largely committed to housing and mixed-use redevelopment — a different landscape than the Chicago pipeline. What remains genuinely open are points of existing electrical capacity, and operators facing real power constraints of their own. These are the starting points for outreach, not sites VTCNZE has secured.

Grid Reference

Potrero Switchyard

A 4.8-acre PG&E-operated 230kV electrical switchyard on Illinois Street — active transmission infrastructure adjacent to (but distinct from) the Potrero Power Station redevelopment, which is fully committed to housing.

Grid Reference

Hunters Point Substation, Evans Ave

PG&E's substation modernization project serving southeast San Francisco, designed to net-zero energy standards. Distinct from the adjacent former Shipyard Superfund site.

BTM Outreach Target

400 Paul Avenue

Novva's 36MW critical-load data center campus in the Bayview — a real operator with a documented PG&E capacity constraint that behind-the-meter storage could help solve.

BTM Outreach Target

Port of San Francisco, Piers 80/92–94

Active maritime cargo terminals with Port-earmarked funds for electrification improvements — a plausible shore-power BTM opportunity as California's clean-port mandates advance.

All locations referenced above are under evaluation as part of early-stage outreach. No site control, land ownership, or interconnection rights are implied or have been established as of this page's publication.
Power To The People

Community ownership isn't the pitch. It's the structure.

Every Stack SPV reserves 40% of distributable profits for its host community, administered through the DigiPie app as Consumer Earned Tokenized Equities. Federal, state, and city tranches follow — with VTCNZE's own share capped at 3% after the RTD licensing fee.

If San Francisco becomes a Stack SPV host, this is the mechanism its residents would actually see — not a grant recipient, but a profit participant.
